NitoAgua

NitoAgua is a side project I built to coordinate drinking-water deliveries in rural southern Chile: a customer posts a request, providers approved by an admin send offers, and the customer accepts one. It is publicly deployed on Next.js and Supabase, and the project is on hold.

1 / 1

How it is built

How it works

  • Ordering needs no account. A three-step form (contact and comuna; volume, urgency and payment method; review), with an optional map pin, posts to a Next.js route. The route validates the body with Zod and stores the request server-side with a random UUID tracking token. A guest follows the request and its offers through that token.
  • Providers get their feed from a server action. It lists pending requests in the comunas they serve, urgent first, and only once an admin has approved them and they are marked available. Row-level security guards the offer side: only approved providers can insert an offer, and a partial unique index allows one active offer per provider per request. An admin, checked against an allowlist table, approves each provider after reviewing their ID, driver's license and vehicle photos.
  • Signed-in customers and providers see new requests and offers without reloading. They get them through Supabase Realtime subscriptions on the requests and offers tables, with a 30-second polling fallback if the channel fails. Guests cannot subscribe, because RLS blocks anonymous reads, so their pages are read on the server with the tracking token. The tracking page refreshes itself every 30 seconds, and the offers page shows new offers on reload.
  • Accepting an offer is one PL/pgSQL function, select_offer, so the step is atomic. It re-checks that the offer is active and the request still pending, accepts that offer, marks the others as filled and assigns the provider. Who may accept (the account owner or the holder of the tracking token) is checked in the TypeScript server action before the call, not inside the function.
  • Two daily Vercel cron jobs, protected by a shared secret, do the clean-up. One marks offers past their deadline as expired. The other marks requests still pending after 4 hours with no active offer as 'no offers'. Between runs, only the offer screen checks the deadline: it greys out expired offers, sorts them last and blocks selecting them. select_offer itself looks at the offer's status, not its deadline.
