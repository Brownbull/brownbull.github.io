Cifra · Chile

Cifra · Chile is a Spanish-language data magazine that explains complex Chilean public-interest cases step by step, linking its figures to their sources and tagging estimates 'ia'. I built it with Claude Code in June 2026 (last update 3 July). AI researched and drafted the threads, checked their sources and ran a legal red-team on six sensitive pages, with me as editor. The deployed site runs no AI. It is a Next.js app with Google sign-in and a small PostgreSQL backend for votes, moderated comments and a newsletter.

How it is built

How it works

  • Each thread is a React page in TypeScript, with its figures kept as typed data in the code. Figures link either to a shared registry of 234 sources (as of September 2026), ranked from the Central Bank down to the press (84 of them), or straight to a document. Estimates carry an 'ia' tag.
  • AI works while the pages are made, not in the running site. The threads were researched and drafted with Claude Code, and a source-check skill stamps each of the 48 published threads with the agent, model and date of its pass. The first site-wide pass (7 June 2026, 37 of the 48 stamps) checked that links were live and pointed to the primary document. On 10 June the skill added a check that each key figure appears in the cited document.
  • Six pages that name people or companies got an adversarial legal review from an AI agent in June 2026. It builds the strongest defamation or privacy claim against the page, then argues the defense, and only the claims that survive become fixes that I approve. It is an editorial check, not legal advice.
  • The backend is small. Reading needs no account. Votes and comments need a Google sign-in: those routes verify the Firebase ID token on the server, and unique constraints in PostgreSQL (through Prisma) allow one vote and one comment per reader per article. The newsletter (double opt-in), the contact form and comment reports work without an account, behind a cooldown or a honeypot and rate limit.
  • Comments are moderated before they appear. A reader's note stays private until they ask to publish it. It then waits as pending until I approve it, either from a local pipeline where an AI flag suggests a verdict or from an admin page limited to an allowlist. Editing a published comment sends it back to pending.
